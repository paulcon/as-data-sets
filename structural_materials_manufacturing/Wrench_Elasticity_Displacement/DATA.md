# Wrench_Elasticity_Displacement: data

Kind: **Python model**. The model is `wrench_model.py` (numpy and scipy), a new implementation of the high-fidelity wrench model of Lam, Zahm, Marzouk and Willcox (2020), written from the paper and from the problem set-up of the authors' MATLAB code, whose code was not copied. The mesh is made by `wrench_mesh.py` (numpy and scipy). `generate_data.py` writes every data file with saved seeds and settings; on the platform where the files were written a rerun reproduces them to the tolerances under "Regenerating".

## Model

**Source.** R. R. Lam, O. Zahm, Y. M. Marzouk, K. E. Willcox, "Multifidelity dimension reduction via active subspaces", *SIAM J. Sci. Comput.* 42(2):A929–A956, 2020, doi:10.1137/18M1214123. The preprint arXiv:1809.05567v2 (7 Jan 2020) was read; page numbers are the preprint's. Example 5.2, "Linear elasticity analysis of a wrench", Sec. 5.2.1–5.2.2, pp. 12–16. The paper's code (footnote 2, p. 13) is wrenchmark, https://gitlab.inria.fr/ozahm/wrenchmark (O. Zahm; MATLAB with the PDE Toolbox; commit 77d9ada361b666336eef9c7666657d32e5d80be2, archive sha256 c80504c323b06df5a04eebdb4aa3e84c269fc76a6302ffb51743093453768321; no licence file). It was read for the geometry, the boundary pieces, the point of interest and the sampling of the field; none of its code is in this directory.

**Equations** (p. 12). div(K : ε(u)) = 0 on Ω ⊂ R², ε(u) = ½(∇u + ∇uᵀ), plane stress, K : ε(u) = E/(1+ν) ε(u) + νE/(1−ν²) trace(ε(u)) I₂, ν = 0.3. Boundary conditions (Fig. 5a, p. 13): u = 0 on the two inner jaw faces (from the jaw hexagon's vertex at (3.95, 0.84) to the head circle, and from its vertex at (2.997, −0.81) to the head circle), a unit downward traction (0, −1) per unit length on the straight top edge of the handle (y = 0.4, −2.338 ≤ x ≤ 1.4), traction free elsewhere. The figure shows the pieces; the edge choice and the traction value are from wrenchmark (`applyBoundaryCondition`: Dirichlet on two edges, Neumann g = (0, −1) on one).

**Geometry** (wrenchmark `initGeo`; nondimensional length, the paper gives no units): union of the rectangle [−2.8, 2.8] × [−0.4, 0.4], the circles of radius 1.4 at (3.5, 0) and 1 at (−3.5, 0), the rectangle [1.4, 2.413] × [−1, 1] and the quadrilateral (−1.75, 0), (−3, 0.85), (−2.7, 0), (−3, −0.85); minus the hexagon of circumradius 1.1 centred at (3.95, −0.26) with a vertex at 30°, the hexagon of circumradius 0.6 centred at (−3.5, 0) with a vertex at 0°, and the circles of radius 1.305 at (1.4, ±1.705) (fillets tangent to the handle and the head). Area 9.272588 (printed by `generate_data.py`).

**Discretization** (p. 13). Galerkin P1 finite elements, E^h piecewise constant (one value per triangle). The paper's mesh had d = 2197 elements (p. 13); the paper does not say how it was made, and wrenchmark makes its mesh with the MATLAB PDE Toolbox (`initMesh`: `generateMesh` with a maximum element size `Hmax`). This data set's mesh (`wrench_mesh.py`, written for it) also has 2197 triangles: boundary nodes at spacing about h = 0.097 on every boundary piece with the corners kept (291 nodes), 953 interior nodes started on an equilateral lattice (spacing reduced until there are enough nodes; the surplus nearest the boundary dropped, so that the count is exactly 2197) and relaxed by 300 truss-spring iterations with fixed boundary nodes (after Persson and Strang, SIAM Rev. 46:329–345, 2004, implemented anew), then a Delaunay triangulation (scipy) restricted to the domain. Angles 29.6°–109.8°; 1244 nodes, 2440 free degrees of freedom. The script checks that the triangulation conforms (every boundary segment is an edge, triangle count 2V_int + V_bnd) and that (−4.1, 0) is a node. No outside mesher is used.

**Inputs** (pp. 12–13). The physical input is X = log E^h ∈ R²¹⁹⁷, X ~ N(0, Σ), Σ_ij = C(c_i, c_j), C(s, t) = exp(−‖s − t‖²₂/l₀), l₀ = 1. The paper says "the center of the elements"; the centres here are the incentres, as in wrenchmark (`incenter`). The paper standardizes, X = Σ^{1/2} X_std, X_std ~ N(0, I_d), and works with H = E[∇f_std ∇f_stdᵀ] (p. 13). Σ is numerically singular (λ₁ = 352.34; its eigenvalues fall below 10⁻¹⁵ λ₁), so wrenchmark samples X = U D^{1/2} ξ with the eigenpairs whose eigenvalue is at least 10 ε λ_max. This data set does the same: Φ = U_r D_r^{1/2}, r = 318 (the count of that rule here), each column signed so that its largest-magnitude entry is positive. The largest dropped eigenvalue is 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ λ₁ (printed by `generate_data.py`), so the truncation error ‖Σ − ΦΦᵀ‖₂ is at the rounding level of Σ. The cut-off itself sits at the rounding level: eigenvalue 319 of Σ is 2.18 × 10⁻¹⁵ λ₁ against a cut-off of 2.22 × 10⁻¹⁵ λ₁ (`generate_data.py`), so another platform may keep 318 or 319; `generate_data.py` always keeps R_KL = 318 (and stops if the rule's count differs from 318 by more than 5). The choice does not matter for the active subspace: the basis fields beyond the 100th carry a fraction 3.1 × 10⁻⁸ of the trace of the gradient matrix, and those beyond the 200th 3.4 × 10⁻¹³ (notebook). Because ∇_ξ f = Φᵀ ∇_X f, the matrix E[∇_ξ f ∇_ξ fᵀ] has the nonzero eigenvalues of the paper's H up to the dropped eigenvalues of Σ, and eigenvector fields on the mesh are Φw.

**Quantity of interest** (p. 13). f = u₂^h(PoI), the vertical displacement at the point of interest, the left vertex (−4.1, 0) of the ring's hexagonal hole (the green dot of Fig. 5a; coordinates from wrenchmark, which takes the nearest mesh node). Nondimensional; negative. At X = 0, f = −1802.300742 (`check_accuracy.py`, check 4). Over the 1000 samples, f ∈ [−20483.3, −399.1], mean −2685.9, median −2150.6 (notebook).

**Gradients** (p. 13, "computed with the adjoint method"). K(E)u = b, K(E)q = ℓ with ℓ the unit vector of the vertical degree of freedom at the PoI (K symmetric, so one sparse LU factorization, `scipy.sparse.linalg.splu`, serves both), ∂f/∂X_k = −E_k q_kᵀ K_k u_k with K_k the element stiffness at E = 1, and ∂f/∂ξ = Φᵀ ∂f/∂X. `gradients.csv` holds ∂f/∂ξ, the gradient with respect to the inputs of `inputs.csv`. The physical gradient ∂f/∂X is not stored (`Wrench.f_and_grad` computes it).

**Sampling design.** M = 1000 independent Monte Carlo draws ξ ~ N(0, I₃₁₈), `numpy.random.default_rng(2020).standard_normal((1000, 318))`. `reference_10000.csv`: 10⁴ further Monte Carlo draws, `default_rng(2021)`, in chunks of 500 rows; H = (1/10⁴) Σ ∇_ξ f ∇_ξ fᵀ, all 318 eigenvalues and eigenvectors 1–5, each signed so that its field Φw has its largest-magnitude entry positive. All samples are within the model's meaning (every E is positive, every solve succeeded).

**Published results** (the high-fidelity active subspace). Fig. 7 right (p. 15): the eigenvalues 1–50 of the 10⁴-sample reference estimator; read from the vector paths of the figure in the arXiv v2 PDF (marker centres, axis ticks −4 … 6 in log₁₀; precision about 0.001 in log₁₀), log₁₀ λ_k for k = 1–5 is 6.674, 5.536, 5.068, 4.341, 4.010, and 2.832, 1.024, −2.354 for k = 10, 20, 50 (all 50 values are in the notebook). p. 14: "a few modes (≈ 5) are sufficient". Fig. 8 (p. 16): modes 1 and 2 as fields on the mesh; by eye, mode 1 is largest on the handle near x ≈ 1.3 and mode 2 is positive near x ≈ 0.3 and negative near x ≈ 2.2. The colour bars have fixed limits, 0 to 1 (mode 1) and −0.8 to 0.8 (mode 2): the colour at the top of the mode-1 bar and those at both ends of the mode-2 bar do not occur in the fields. Read from the colours (each pixel of the reference panels matched to the nearest colour of its bar, to about 0.02), the reference fields reach about 0.92 (mode 1) and about +0.77 and −0.55 (mode 2). The paper's multifidelity results (Fig. 7 left; relative error 0.4 at γ = 4 for MF and γ = 7 for SF, p. 14) use the 423-element low-fidelity model, which is not part of this data set.

**What the notebook finds.** From the 1000 gradients: λ₁ = 4.795 × 10⁶, λ₁/λ₂ = 13.06, λ₂/λ₃ = 3.06, λ₃/λ₄ = 6.18, λ₄/λ₅ = 2.0; n = 3 is chosen (second gap, bootstrap distance 0.046 at k = 3 against 0.1 at k = 4, left-out share 0.008, cubic LOO R² 0.986 against 0.932 for n = 1 and 0.9863 for n = 4). The 10⁴-sample reference lies within −0.065 (k = 13) to +0.031 (k = 44) of the paper's 50 eigenvalues in log₁₀ (mean +0.001); 10 of the 50 differ by more than 0.02. Mode 1 is a positive bump at the root of the handle (maximum 0.926 at (1.36, −0.02)), mode 2 is positive mid-handle (0.777 at x = 0.2) and negative in the neck of the head (−0.556 at x = 2.04), as in Fig. 8, and the reference fields' extrema (0.915; +0.752, −0.589) are within 0.04 of the values read from Fig. 8 (notebook). The reference eigenvalues' differences from the paper's, k = 13 included, are of the size expected from Monte Carlo error (notebook).

## Regenerating

```bash
python generate_data.py --check               # writes every data file to a temporary directory and compares
python generate_data.py --out DIR --check     # the same, keeping the files in DIR (not this directory)
python generate_data.py --n 100 --check       # quick: mesh, basis and the first 100 samples, no reference run
python check_accuracy.py                      # the numbers marked below
python generate_data.py --out .               # rewrites the shipped files (only on purpose)
```

`--out` is required without `--check` (with `--check` it defaults to a temporary directory, removed afterwards) and may not be a folder inside this directory; `--check` and `--n` also refuse this directory itself. `--check` prints the differences listed below and the largest of them; it exits with code 1 only on a structural mismatch: a missing file, a different shape or NaN pattern, a different set of triangles or boundary segments, or inputs that differ from the shipped ones by more than 10⁻⁸ (relative). One process, no multiprocessing; set `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` on a shared machine. The shipped files were written on 2026-11-08 in a Linux x86-64 container (Python 3.13.18, numpy 2.5.4, scipy 1.18.1) with `python generate_data.py --out .`. In the repository's `.venv` (macOS, Python 3.11.16, numpy 2.4.6, scipy 1.17.1; 2026-11-09), `generate_data.py --out DIR --check` took 121 s (mesh 13 s, eigendecomposition of Σ 4 s, 1000 solves with adjoint gradients 11.9 s, 11.9 ms each, the 10⁴-sample reference 91 s) and printed: the same 2197 triangles and boundary segments, nodes within 3.1 × 10⁻¹⁵, eigenvalues of Σ within 4.3 × 10⁻¹⁵ λ₁, the compared columns of Φ within 3.2 × 10⁻⁸, outputs within 8.9 × 10⁻⁸ and gradients within 1.4 × 10⁻⁷ (relative), the reference eigenvalues within 1.2 × 10⁻⁷ and eigenvectors within 8.7 × 10⁻⁸; the largest, 1.4 × 10⁻⁷ (gradients, relative row 2-norm), is the same in a rerun on 2026-11-12 (182 s). scipy's Delaunay (Qhull) returns the triangles in a different order there (768 of the 2197 keep their index), so `--check` compares the triangles as a set and the rows of Φ after mapping the new element order onto the shipped one; `--out .` on another platform can write the same mesh in another element order, with the rows of Φ in the matching order. Expect about 2 to 6 minutes on one core. The compared measures are: mesh nodes absolute, eigenvalues of Σ relative to λ₁, the columns of Φ whose eigenvalue is at least 10⁻⁸ λ₁ relative per column (2-norm), inputs and outputs relative, gradients relative (row 2-norm), reference eigenvalues 1–50 relative and eigenvectors 1–5 in the 2-norm. Columns of Φ with eigenvalues below 10⁻⁸ λ₁ are not compared: with eigenvalues down to order 10⁻¹⁵ λ₁ they are at the rounding level of Σ and differ between eigensolvers. Recomputing Φ with the LAPACK drivers `evr` and `ev` (`scipy.linalg.eigh`) instead of numpy's changed the compared columns by at most 3.2 × 10⁻⁸ and the outputs and gradients of the first 30 samples by at most 4.4 × 10⁻⁸ and 7.2 × 10⁻⁸ (relative); no shipped script makes this comparison ("external run, not reproducible from the repository"). The mesh is the most fragile step: Delaunay triangulation of the relaxed nodes is exact for given coordinates, but a platform whose floating-point results differ in the last bits could, in principle, flip a near-degenerate pair of triangles, which `--check` reports as a mesh difference.

## Accuracy

`check_accuracy.py` recomputes the numbers marked with its name; in the repository's `.venv` it took 11 s (2026-11-09). The notebook prints no total run time; `jupyter nbconvert --execute` ran it in 43 s and 68 s in `.venv` (2026-11-09).

- **Gradients** (`check_accuracy.py`, check 1). Adjoint against complex-step derivatives (step 10⁻³⁰, which has no truncation error) along 30 random directions at rows 0–9: largest relative error 2.5 × 10⁻⁸, median 2.6 × 10⁻¹⁰; against central differences with step 10⁻³: largest 1.4 × 10⁻⁴ (truncation error of the differences). The notebook re-runs 5 samples and matches the files to the accuracy of the solves (relative differences of order 10⁻¹¹ in `.venv`).
- **Scaling identity** (check 2). Σ_k ∂f/∂X_k = −f exactly (u scales as 1/E for a uniform change of E): satisfied to a relative 6.3 × 10⁻¹¹ at rows 0–9 (the notebook prints a value of order 10⁻¹¹ at rows 0–4).
- **Linear solves** (check 3). Normwise backward error of the sparse LU solves of order 10⁻¹⁶; condition number cond₁(K) = 3.2 × 10⁷ at row 0. Their product bounds the relative accuracy of displacements and gradients at a few times 10⁻⁹, consistent with the complex-step comparison.
- **Discretization** (check 4). Uniform refinement of the shipped mesh (each triangle split into four, children keep their parent's modulus, polygonal boundary kept): f at X = 0 is −1802.300742, −1838.610714 and −1850.533878 on 2197, 8788 and 35 152 triangles, so the shipped mesh is 2.9 % below an Aitken extrapolation in magnitude (P1 triangles are stiff in bending). The eigenvalues 1–5 from the first 200 samples rise by 4.1 %–5.4 % from the shipped mesh to 8788 triangles. The paper's mesh has the same element type and count, so its discretization error is of the same kind; the differences between this data set and the paper (eigenvalues 1–50 within −0.065 to +0.031 in log₁₀, notebook) are of a similar size, and of the size expected from Monte Carlo error alone (see below).
- **Field truncation** (`generate_data.py`). Largest dropped eigenvalue of Σ: of order 10⁻¹⁵ λ₁(Σ), the rounding level; the truncation error ‖Σ − ΦΦᵀ‖₂ is of that order.

## How these files relate to the paper

- **Not the paper's computation.** The paper's results come from wrenchmark in MATLAB (PDE Toolbox mesh with Hmax set in the code; the paper does not state it). The data here are an independent numpy/scipy computation of the same problem on a different 2197-triangle mesh. The geometry, boundary pieces, point of interest, field covariance, incentres and truncation rule follow wrenchmark; the paper states the equations, ν, l₀, d, the QoI and the standardization.
- **Agreement.** The 10⁴-sample reference eigenvalues follow the paper's Fig. 7 reference over all 50 indices to −0.065 … +0.031 in log₁₀ (notebook); the first five are (paper 4.721 × 10⁶, 3.436 × 10⁵, 1.169 × 10⁵, 2.193 × 10⁴, 1.023 × 10⁴; here 4.524 × 10⁶, 3.569 × 10⁵, 1.125 × 10⁵, 2.245 × 10⁴, 1.047 × 10⁴, that is −4.2, +3.9, −3.8, +2.4, +2.3 %). The leading two eigenvector fields have the shape, the extremum positions and the amplitudes of Fig. 8 (reference extrema 0.915; +0.752, −0.589 against about 0.92; +0.77, −0.55 read from the figure's colours; notebook), as expected for Φw with unit-norm w, the quantity wrenchmark's tutorial plots (`UU = Sigma12*UU`); and the dominant gap (λ₁/λ₂ = 13.06 here) is the paper's.
- **Where it does not match.** (1) The eigenvalues are close, not equal: the largest difference is −0.065 in log₁₀ at k = 13, and 10 of the 50 differences exceed 0.02, far above the precision with which the figure was read (about 0.001). All of this is of the size expected from Monte Carlo error. The bootstrap of the 1000 gradients, scaled by √2/√10 for the difference of two independent 10⁴-sample estimates, gives an expected standard deviation of the log₁₀ difference of 0.016 (median over k = 1–50; range 0.013–0.028). Against it the difference at k = 13 is −2.3 standard deviations, 3 of the 50 lie beyond 2 (largest 2.6, at k = 38), and 11.9 differences above 0.02 are expected against the 10 observed (notebook). This bootstrap scaling, if anything, understates the scatter: two further independent 10⁴-sample estimates on this mesh (`generate_data.reference` with seeds 3031 and 3032) differ from `reference_10000.csv` and from each other by up to 0.068 in log₁₀ over k = 1–50 (root mean square 0.024–0.027), with 20–22 of the 50 differences above 0.02, more than the paper's eigenvalues differ from `reference_10000.csv` (root mean square 0.019, 10 above 0.02). No shipped script makes these two estimates (external run, not reproducible from the repository). For λ₁…λ₅ (−4.2 to +3.9 %) the 1000-sample 95 % bootstrap half-widths are 16.8–21.9 %, roughly 5.2–6.9 % when scaled to 10⁴ samples (notebook). The different mesh adds to this (λ₁…λ₅ move by 4.1–5.4 % under one refinement; `check_accuracy.py`, check 4). Nothing points to a modelling difference, but this data set is not a reproduction of the authors' numbers. (2) The paper reads "a few modes (≈ 5)" from the spectrum (p. 14); the notebook finds no gap after k = 5 and chooses n = 3.
- **Not reproduced.** The low-fidelity model (423-element coarse mesh, field averaged over coarse elements), the multifidelity estimator and Fig. 7 left; the ONERA M6 example (Sec. 5.3) and the analytical examples (Sec. 5.1).
- **Open questions (only the authors can settle).** (1) The PDE Toolbox `Hmax` that gave the 2197-element mesh (wrenchmark's default is 0.032; its tutorial uses 0.15, 0.22 and 0.1, and a comment there lists 0.5, 0.15 and 0.08). (2) Whether the published SISC Figs. 7–8 differ from the preprint's (only the preprint was read).

## Unknowns

- The paper's mesh (only its element count is stated); the mesh here is a different one with the same count.
- "Center of the elements" (p. 13): incentres are used, as in wrenchmark.
- Units: none in the paper; everything is nondimensional (unit traction, E = exp(X)).
- The truncation of Σ: the paper writes a full-rank Σ^{1/2}; the truncation (r = 318, eigenvalues ≥ 10 ε λ_max) follows wrenchmark.
