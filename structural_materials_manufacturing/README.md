# Structural, materials and additive manufacturing

Structural mechanics, materials models and additive manufacturing.

Each data set below has a short summary of the science: what the model computes, what varies, and what the active subspace shows. The numbers come from the data set's notebook, which has the details and the comparison with the paper. The [top-level README](../README.md#data-sets) has the paper links and the kind of each data set (stored data or Python model).

### [Epoxy_MD_Force_Field_Sensitivity](Epoxy_MD_Force_Field_Sensitivity/)

Edeling et al. ran LAMMPS molecular dynamics of a cross-linked epoxy resin at 500 Monte Carlo points of 103 force-field parameters, the coefficients of the bonded and Lennard-Jones terms, each uniform within ±15 % of its default, with 20 replica runs per point. The notebook analyses the replica means of two elastic outputs: the stiffness component C₁₁, which the paper uses as its Young's modulus, and the Poisson ratio. Without gradients, it estimates the subspace from local linear gradients and finds one dominant eigenvalue followed by a flat tail (λ₂/λ₃ between 1.04 and 1.27); the size of the first gap depends on the estimator's neighbourhood size (λ₁/λ₂ from 42.9 to 739.1 for the stiffness and from 81.5 to 2144.8 for the Poisson ratio), so the notebook calibrates the trailing eigenvalues against three nulls: replica noise alone, an exact one-dimensional ridge, and that ridge with shuffled residuals. A cubic in the one active variable predicts both outputs with leave-one-out R² 0.9615 and 0.9606, no second direction is detectable, and the leading inputs agree with the paper's Fig. 5 (10 and 9 of the top 10).

### [Powder_Bed_Fusion_Residual_Stress](Powder_Bed_Fusion_Residual_Stress/)

Guo and Kramer simulate the fusing of one 50 µm Ti–6Al–4V powder layer by a single electron-beam scan with sequential thermal and mechanical Abaqus models. The output is the maximum von Mises residual stress σ_max after cooling, the limit state of a risk-constrained design problem over scanning speed and beam power, with the preheating temperature, yield strength, elastic modulus and density uncertain. From the authors' 120 training runs (values only), a global quadratic gives one dominant direction (λ₁/λ₂ = 28.1, bootstrap median 14.6), made mostly of beam power (+0.920) against scanning speed (−0.342), and a degree-4 polynomial in the single active variable predicts σ_max with leave-one-out R² 0.9214. The structure is one-dimensional but kinked: 57 of the 120 runs reach the yield strength, and the active variable separates them and tracks the temperature at the end of the scan track (correlation 0.958). The same direction comes out of the 63 runs below yield and of a quadratic capped at the yield strength, and the unseen validation runs confirm its small part in the four uncertain inputs.

### [Steel_Arch_Inplane_Buckling](Steel_Arch_Inplane_Buckling/)

Closed-form formulas of Pi, Bradford and Uy give the in-plane elastic buckling load of a shallow circular steel I-section arch under uniform compression; Nguyen used them to compute Sobol' indices for a fixed and a pin-ended arch. Seven inputs vary uniformly by about ±7 % to ±12 %: the arch length, the included angle, Young's modulus and four cross-section dimensions, and a numpy rebuild of the model gives exact gradients at 1000 samples per support condition. The fixed arch has a one-dimensional active subspace (λ₁/λ₂ = 401.7) along which the load is nearly linear (a quadratic in y₁ has leave-one-out R² 0.99837); the direction weights the section depth most, then the arch length, the modulus and the flange dimensions. The pin-ended arch, whose formula switches branch inside the input box, has a clear two-dimensional subspace (λ₁/λ₂ = 30.9, λ₂/λ₃ = 53.1): its first direction is close to the fixed arch's, and its second, led by the included angle, follows the arch's slenderness. The second active variable raises the leave-one-out R² of a quadratic from 0.98867 to 0.99851.

### [Two_Material_Thermal_Compliance](Two_Material_Thermal_Compliance/)

A square plate made of two conducting materials is heated by a fixed source, and the design inputs are the fractions θ₁, …, θ₅₁₂ of the better conductor in each of the 512 triangles of a finite element mesh. The output is the thermal energy (heat compliance) E(θ), which Luneau's design problem constrains by E ≤ E_max = 1.35. With 1500 exact adjoint gradients at uniformly random designs, the gradient covariance has one dominant eigenvalue (λ₁/λ₂ = 551.6, holding 95.82 % of the trace), and its eigenvector is a one-signed weighting of the elements that is largest on an annulus around the centre. E is close to a linear function of the single active variable (leave-one-out R² 0.98815 for a straight line, 0.98831 for a quadratic), which is what lets the paper replace the 512-dimensional constraint by a one-dimensional surrogate.

### [Wrench_Elasticity_Displacement](Wrench_Elasticity_Displacement/)

Lam et al. study a wrench, clamped at the inner faces of its jaw and pushed down along its handle, modelled by plane-stress linear elasticity with P1 finite elements on 2197 triangles. The inputs are the log Young's modulus on every triangle, a correlated Gaussian field written with 318 independent standard normal coordinates, and the output is the vertical displacement of a point on the ring at the far end of the handle. From 1000 exact adjoint gradients, the first eigenvalue is 13.06 times the second, with a second gap λ₃/λ₄ = 6.18, and three eigenvalues leave out 0.8 % of the sum. The leading eigenvector is a stiffening of the root of the handle, where the bending moment is largest and the section narrowest. A cubic has leave-one-out R² 0.932 in one active variable and 0.986 in three.
