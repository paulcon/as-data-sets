# PyGEM_Glacier_Mass_Balance: data

Kind: **Python model**. The model is `pygem_mb_model.py` (numpy only), a re-implementation of the mass-balance code of PyGEM v0.1.0, the version the paper used. `generate_data.py` draws the inputs with saved seeds and writes the six study CSVs; a rerun reproduces them to the tolerances under "Regenerating". The model's own inputs (glacier bins and ERA5 forcing) are stored data, reduced from OGGM sample-data files by `prepare_inputs.py`.

**This is not the paper's glacier or forcing.** Rounce et al. calibrate every glacier of High Mountain Asia with ERA-Interim and RGI v6.0. Those inputs could not be obtained for this data set, so the paper's model is run on Hintereisferner (RGI60-11.00897, Ötztal Alps, Austria) with ERA5 forcing, from the OGGM sample-data repository. The equations, options and calibrated parameters are the paper's; every number is this glacier's.

## Model

**Source.** D. R. Rounce, T. Khurana, M. B. Short, R. Hock, D. E. Shean, D. J. Brinkerhoff, "Quantifying parameter uncertainty in a large-scale glacier evolution model using Bayesian inference: application to High Mountain Asia", *J. Glaciol.* 66(256):175–187, 2020, doi:10.1017/jog.2019.91 (publisher PDF, open access, CC BY 4.0). Model: Sec. 2, pp. 176–177; priors: Sec. 3.2, pp. 178–179; identifiability: Secs. 3.4 and 4.3, pp. 179 and 182. Code: PyGEM v0.1.0 (Data availability statement, p. 186), https://github.com/drounce/PyGEM, tag `v0.1`, commit 4d710538dae04fc178a383cafaae04198e3e88dd (26 Dec 2019), MIT licence.

**Equations** (as printed, pp. 176–177). Monthly climatic mass balance b_clim = a + c + R on 10 m bins (Eq. 1), glacier-wide B = Σ b_clim,bin S_bin / S (Eq. 2). Ablation a = f_snow/ice/firn · T · n for T ≥ 0 (Eq. 3; in the code snow melts first, then firn or ice with the remaining degree-days). Temperature T_m,bin = T_m,GCM + lr_GCM (z_ref − z_GCM) + lr_glac (z_bin − z_ref) + T_bias (Eq. 4), z_ref the median elevation. Accumulation c = δ P (Eq. 5), δ = 1 for T ≤ T_snow − 1, 0 for T ≥ T_snow + 1, printed as 0.5 + (T − T_snow)/2 in between (Eq. 6). **Eq. 6 as printed has the wrong sign** (the snow fraction would rise with temperature and jump at both ends); PyGEM v0.1.0 uses 0.5 + (T − T_snow)/2 as the rain fraction, i.e. δ = 0.5 − (T − T_snow)/2, and the port follows the code. Precipitation P_m,bin = P_m,GCM k_p (1 + d_prec (z_bin − z_ref)) (Eq. 7). Refreeze potential R = −0.0069 T_a + 0.000096 m w.e. from the weighted annual mean temperature, reset every October (Eq. 8); melt refreezes until the potential is exhausted, refreeze cannot exceed the snow depth and cannot be negative (pp. 176–177). v0.1's code also lets rain refreeze (limit: melt plus rain), and the port follows it. f_snow = 0.7 f_ice, f_firn = (f_snow + f_ice)/2, d_prec = 0.01 % m⁻¹, T_snow = 1 °C (Sec. 2.1, p. 177). Annual Huss–Hock mass redistribution (p. 177).

**The port** (`pygem_mb_model.py`) follows `runmassbalance`, `massredistributionHuss`, `massredistributioncurveHuss`, `surfacetypebinsinitial`, `surfacetypebinsannual` and `surfacetypeDDFdict` of PyGEM v0.1's `pygemfxns_massbalance.py`, written anew in numpy (the code is MIT; its licence is kept in `LICENSE-PyGEM.txt`). Options are those of v0.1's `pygem_input.py`: monthly step; `option_temp2bins` 1 with `option_adjusttemp_surfelev` 1 (temperatures follow surface lowering); `option_prec2bins` 1 with `option_preclimit` 1 (precipitation on the uppermost 25 % of the bins decays exponentially when the glacier spans more than 1000 m, which applies here); `option_accumulation` 2 (Eq. 6); `option_ablation` 1 (monthly temperature); `option_ddf_firn` 1; `option_refreezing` 2 (Woodward, Eq. 8), reset month 10; initial surface type snow/firn above and ice below the median elevation, updated annually from the 5-year running mean of each bin's annual climatic mass balance; `option_massredistribution` 1 with the parabolic shape (`option_glaciershape` 1), advance threshold 5 m, terminus 20 %; ice density 900 kg m⁻³; tolerance 1e-12. Left out: frontal ablation (the glacier ends on land, so v0.1 computes none) and the off-glacier bookkeeping, which does not enter B. v0.1's `option_shift_elevbins_20m = 1` is not applied: it shifts the Huss hypsometry tables by 20 m when v0.1 reads them (`pygemfxns_modelsetup.py`), and the bins here come from the consensus thickness and SRTM instead. Reproduced quirk: v0.1 applies the 87.5 % floor on the upper-glacier precipitation to the columns of the first model year in every year, so the floor acts in the first year only; the port does the same. The calibration used an evolving area (`option_areaconstant=0` in v0.1's `run_calibration.py`), and so does the port by default.

**Quantity of interest.** B (m w.e. a⁻¹) for January 2000–December 2017: total mass change over the 216 months divided by the initial area and by 18 years, as `run_calibration.py` of v0.1 computes it (`mb_mwea`). The paper's period is "2000 to 2018" (p. 176); the 18 calendar years are this data set's reading. At the nominal point (k_p = 1, T_bias = 0, f_snow = 4.1, d_prec = 10⁻⁴ m⁻¹, T_snow = 1 °C) B = −1.4510 m w.e. a⁻¹ and the area shrinks from 8.0325 to 6.6287 km² (notebook).

**Glacier and forcing (substitutes; this data set's choice).**

- Glacier: Hintereisferner, RGI60-11.00897. Bins from the consensus ice thickness of Farinotti et al. (2019, doi:10.1038/s41561-019-0300-3) on a 25 m UTM 32N grid (pixels with thickness > 0: 8.0325 km²; RGI area 8.036 km²) and SRTM elevations of February 2000 resampled bilinearly onto that grid, grouped in 10 m bins (bin k: 10k ≤ z < 10(k+1) m, centre 10k + 5). Median elevation 3060.0 m (RGI v5: 3050 m). Volume 0.5779 km³. The paper used RGI v6.0 bands with the Huss and Farinotti (2012) thickness (p. 177).
- Forcing: ERA5 (Hersbach et al. 2020, doi:10.1002/qj.3803) monthly means at the nearest 0.25° cell (46.75 N, 10.75 E; cell height 2425.7 m from the geopotential), 2 m temperature and total precipitation; monthly precipitation = tp × days in month. The paper used ERA-Interim at 0.5° (p. 177).
- Lapse rate: −6.5 K km⁻¹ for both lapse rates (v0.1's `pygem_input.py` defaults `lrgcm`, `lrglac`). The paper derived them from pressure-level temperatures (p. 176), which the sample data do not contain.
- No bias correction; T_bias and k_p play that role, as in the paper.

**Inputs.** The paper's priors are unbounded: f_snow ~ N_T(4.1, 1.5, 0, ∞) mm w.e. d⁻¹ °C⁻¹ (Eq. 16, p. 178, from Braithwaite 2008); T_bias ~ N(μ_reg, σ_reg) (Eq. 17) and k_p ~ Γ(α, β) (Eqs. 18–20, p. 179; β a rate, as in PyMC) with the regional values of High Mountain Asia in the supplement (Figs. S1–S2, not used). Eq. 18 is printed as β = μ_reg/σ_reg; with α = β μ_reg (Eq. 19) that makes the prior mean μ_reg and its variance σ_reg, so σ_reg there is a variance (or Eq. 18 should read β = μ_reg/σ_reg²); v0.1's `pygem_input.py` stores (α, β) directly. The box is this data set's choice and contains the regional prior means of k_p; it is only loosely tied to the priors: the 22 regional k_p gamma priors in v0.1's `pygem_input.py` have 97.5 % quantiles of 2.51–5.95 (median 4.43), so [0.5, 3.5] cuts off prior mass above 3.5 for many regions (median 7.5 %, up to 40.6 %), and the regional T_bias priors reach μ + 2σ = 5.85 °C (`check_accuracy.py`, check 7). Samples are uniform on the box:

| name | symbol | units | nominal | box | basis | study |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `k_p` | k_p | – | 1 | [0.5, 3.5] | contains the 22 regional prior means α/β in v0.1's `pygem_input.py` (0.74–3.35), the Huss–Hock range 0.8–2.0 (p. 186), and k_p = 1 and 3.1 of p. 179 | m3, m5 |
| `T_bias` | T_bias | °C | 0 | [−2, 2] | ±2 °C about no correction; the median regional prior sd in `pygem_input.py` is 0.83 °C; Fig. 3e (p. 180) draws −2 to 2 | m3, m5 |
| `f_snow` | f_snow | mm w.e. d⁻¹ °C⁻¹ | 4.1 | [1.1, 7.1] | μ ± 2σ of Eq. 16 (p. 178) | m3, m5 |
| `d_prec` | d_prec | m⁻¹ | 1e-4 | [0, 2e-4] | 0 to twice the fixed 0.01 % m⁻¹ (p. 177) | m5 |
| `T_snow` | T_snow | °C | 1 | [0, 2] | the fixed 1 °C (p. 177) ± 1 °C | m5 |

The paper fixes d_prec and T_snow (p. 177); freeing them in study m5 is this data set's choice. In m3 they are held at 10⁻⁴ m⁻¹ and 1 °C.

**Gradients.** dB/dx with respect to the physical inputs, by the complex step (step 10⁻³⁰ × max(|x_i|, 10⁻⁴)): every branch of the model (melt only above 0 °C, melt and refreeze limits, surface types, retreat, advance) is decided on the real part, so the result is the exact derivative of the branch the model takes at x (to rounding), which is not always the slope of the response. B is piecewise smooth: it jumps by small amounts where a branch changes (largest found 2.8 × 10⁻⁴ m w.e. a⁻¹; `check_accuracy.py`, check 3), and the gradient does not see the jumps. The jumps add up to about 1 % of the change of B across the box and make the branch derivative an outlier at a few points (Accuracy, checks 5 and 6); the active subspace is not affected.

**Sampling design.** Two independent Monte Carlo designs of M = 1000 draws each, uniform on the box: `numpy.random.default_rng(601).random((1000, 3))` (m3) and `default_rng(602).random((1000, 5))` (m5), mapped linearly to the box. Every draw is inside the box and keeps ice through 2017: the final area ranges over 0.871–9.714 km² (m3) and 1.211–9.684 km² (m5), from 8.0325 km²; B ranges over −3.519 to 2.410 (m3) and −3.471 to 2.373 m w.e. a⁻¹ (m5). No draw grows by more than 25 % in area, and none reaches the lowest bin of the table (the model raises an error if one does).

**Published results** (no active subspace in the paper). The parameters are non-identifiable from one observation per glacier; three settings (T_bias, k_p, f_snow) = (−2 °C, 1, 4.1), (0 °C, 3.1, 2.6), (0 °C, 7.4, 5.6) match the observation of RGI60-13.26360 (p. 179); B against T_bias for nine (k_p, f_snow) pairs (Fig. 2, p. 179); mean posterior correlations k_p–T_bias 0.39 and k_p–f_snow 0.33, and a negative T_bias–f_snow correlation (pp. 182–183).

**What the notebook finds.** m3: λ = 5.111, 0.173, 0.0795; λ₁/λ₂ = 29.6 (bootstrap 27.5–32.0), λ₂/λ₃ = 2.17; n = 1; w₁ = (0.4886, −0.5535, −0.6745) for (k_p, T_bias, f_snow), box-normalized (normalized activity scores 0.239, 0.306, 0.455); leave-one-out R² of a line, quadratic and cubic in y₁: 0.96815, 0.96868, 0.97204; quadratic in (y₁, y₂): 0.98830. m5: λ₁/λ₂ = 30.33, w₁ = (0.4988, −0.5489, −0.6688, −0.0022, 0.0501), 0.71° from the m3 direction in its first three entries; d_prec and T_snow are nearly inactive. **The box sets the size of the gap and the box-normalized weights**: with 300 new draws on a wider box (k_p [0.5, 6], T_bias [−3, 3] °C) and on a narrower one (k_p [0.8, 2], T_bias [−1, 1] °C, f_snow [2.6, 5.6]), λ₁/λ₂ = 25.8 and 115.2, w₁ = (0.613, −0.647, −0.454) and (0.42, −0.562, −0.712), normalized activity scores (0.376, 0.418, 0.206) and (0.176, 0.316, 0.508). The one-dimensional structure holds on all three boxes (cubic in y₁, leave-one-out R² 0.9555 and 0.9924), and the direction per physical unit, w₁/h normalized (h the half-widths), is stable: (0.674, −0.573, −0.466) on the shipped box, 3.7° and 1.4° from it on the other two. With a synthetic observation (B at the nominal point, sd 0.31 m w.e. a⁻¹) and the box as a uniform prior, likelihood weights on the 1000 runs give posterior correlations +0.545, +0.282 and −0.535 for k_p–T_bias, k_p–f_snow and T_bias–f_snow: the paper's signs; k_p–f_snow is of similar size to the paper's mean +0.33, k_p–T_bias is not (paper +0.39). These depend on three choices of this data set: the uniform box as prior, B_obs at the nominal point, and σ = 0.31.

## Regenerating

```bash
python generate_data.py --out DIR                  # writes the six study CSVs to DIR
python generate_data.py --out DIR --check          # the safe mode; DIR outside this directory; compares with the shipped files
python check_accuracy.py --pygem PYGEM_V01_DIR     # the numbers marked below (check 1 needs PyGEM v0.1 and pandas)
python check_accuracy.py --priors PYGEM_V01_DIR    # checks 2-7 without running PyGEM (check 7 parses pygem_input.py)
python prepare_inputs.py --src OGGM_SAMPLE_DATA --out DIR --check   # the stored model inputs (needs netCDF4, rasterio)
```

`generate_data.py` needs only numpy; one process. `--out DIR --check` is the safe mode. `--out` may never be a folder inside this directory, and may be this directory itself only without `--check` (a full rewrite of the shipped files); `prepare_inputs.py` refuses this directory and every folder in it. The shipped CSVs were written on 2026-11-03 in a Linux container by `generate_data.py --out .` (here `.` is this directory). In the repository's `.venv` (macOS, Python 3.11.16, numpy 2.4.6; 2026-11-09), `generate_data.py --out DIR --check` took 222 s (85 ms per m3 draw and 126 ms per m5 draw, with gradients) and passed: the inputs are identical, and the outputs and gradients differ from the shipped files by rounding (of order 10⁻¹⁴ and 10⁻¹² of each column's largest magnitude). Tolerances: 1e-15 relative for the inputs and 1e-9 of each column's largest magnitude for the outputs and gradients (between platforms rounding differences of this order are expected, and a draw lying within that distance of a branch change, improbable, could move by up to the jump size 3 × 10⁻⁴).

## Accuracy

- **Port against PyGEM v0.1** (`check_accuracy.py`, check 1). PyGEM v0.1's `runmassbalance` (commit 4d71053, run with pandas 3.1.0 and numpy 2.5.4; its `pygem_input.py` reads a High Mountain Asia table at import, which the check replaces by an empty table because `runmassbalance` does not use it), fed the same bins and ERA5 series, and the port agree to rounding at 12 points: largest difference in B of order 10⁻¹⁵ m w.e. a⁻¹, and the final areas agree to the printed six decimals: the nominal point, the three settings of p. 179, the box corners (0.5, 2, 7.1) (area falls to 0.54 km²) and (3.5, −2, 1.1) (advance to 10.11 km²), (5, −3, 2) (advance to 11.21 km²), two m5 points with d_prec and T_snow at their box ends, and two constant-area runs.
- **Gradients.** At five rows per study the complex-step gradients agree with central differences (step 10⁻⁷ × box width) to below 10⁻⁹ (m3) and 4.2 × 10⁻⁷ (m5), relative, box-normalized (notebook); `generate_data.py` repeats this on the first 50 draws: median 4.5 × 10⁻¹⁰, largest 1.5 × 10⁻⁶ (m3); median 6.5 × 10⁻¹⁰, largest 4.2 × 10⁻⁷ (m5). At the nominal point (`check_accuracy.py`, check 2) the agreement per input is 1.9 × 10⁻¹⁰ to 3.2 × 10⁻⁷ at step 10⁻⁷, but up to 1.1 × 10⁻² at step 10⁻³: larger steps straddle the model's small jumps.
- **Jumps** (`check_accuracy.py`, check 3). Along T_bias ∈ [−0.5, 0.5] °C at k_p = 1.5, f_snow = 4.1 (2001 points), the largest departure of one step of B from the local trend is 2.8 × 10⁻⁴ m w.e. a⁻¹; 167 of 2000 steps depart by more than 10⁻⁵. B spans −1.3797 to −0.7392 over that scan.
- **Branch derivative against the slope of B** (`check_accuracy.py`, checks 5 and 6). Along 481-point scans over the box (other inputs nominal), the complex-step derivative departs by more than 5 % from its running median (11 points) at 6 points for k_p (1.2 %) and 5 for T_snow (1.0 %); the most extreme, at k_p = 1.94375, is −0.6896 against a trend of +0.7851, and central differences with steps 10⁻⁹, 10⁻⁸, 10⁻⁷ give −0.6896, −0.6896, −0.6906 (the model's branch, not a coding error). The jumps add up: the integral of dB/dk_p over the scan is 2.3388 against B(3.5) − B(0.5) = 2.3655 (−1.1 %); for T_snow 0.1107 against 0.11185 (−1.0 %); for d_prec, which changes B by only −0.00099451 over its box, −0.0013965 (the jumps dominate). On all 1000 rows of study m3, against a smoothed central difference (step 0.005 × box width, which spans many jumps and follows the slope of the response), the relative difference (box-normalized 2-norm) has median 0.77 %, exceeds 10 % at 30 rows and 50 % at 2; the smallest direction cosine is 0.9935. The active subspace is unaffected: λ₁/λ₂ = 29.55 from the smoothed differences against 29.60, and w₁ changes by at most 1.3 × 10⁻³ per entry.
- **Least-squares slope against the mean gradient** (notebook). Box-normalized, m3: slope (1.131, −1.281, −1.527) against mean gradient (1.113, −1.231, −1.491), cosine 0.99995; m5: cosine 0.99982, with the largest difference for the nearly inactive d_prec (−0.035 against −0.005), where noise and the jumps dominate.
- **Check 7** (`check_accuracy.py --priors` or `--pygem`): the priors of v0.1's `pygem_input.py`, parsed as data. k_p gamma means 0.738–3.354 over the 22 regions; 97.5 % quantiles 2.51–5.95 (median 4.43); prior mass above k_p = 3.5: median 7.5 %, largest 40.6 %. T_bias: means −0.60 to +2.27 °C, sds 0.33–1.79 (median 0.83); μ + 2σ up to 5.85 °C; prior mass inside [−2, 2] °C: median 97.9 %, smallest 43.2 %.
- **Check 4** (`check_accuracy.py`): the three settings of p. 179 give B = −0.3164, +1.0384, +2.8277 m w.e. a⁻¹ for Hintereisferner (also printed in the notebook); they are equifinal only for the paper's glacier.

`check_accuracy.py` (checks 2–6) took 171 s in the repository's `.venv` on 2026-11-09 (check 6 makes 6000 model calls); check 1 was run on 2026-11-03 (PyGEM v0.1 at commit 4d71053, pandas 3.1.0, numpy 2.5.4) and its output is unchanged, since the model is unchanged. The notebook calls the model about 530 times without gradients (441 for the Fig. 2 analogue, the rest for the checks) and 600 times with gradients (the box-sensitivity check); the committed run in the repository's `.venv` printed 69.6 s (2026-11-09).

## How these files relate to the paper

- **Not the paper's computation.** The paper ran PyGEM v0.1.0 with PyMC MCMC on about 95,000 glaciers of High Mountain Asia forced by ERA-Interim (pp. 177–180). The data here are the same model (ported and checked against v0.1 itself) on one Alpine glacier with ERA5, a constant lapse rate and a consensus thickness, over a box this data set chose; the paper reports no result for this glacier and no active subspace.
- **Code version.** The tag `v0.1` (26 Dec 2019) is the closest public state to "PyGEMv0.1.0" (p. 186). In that tag, `run_calibration.py` calls `runmassbalance` without the `glacier_gcm_tempstd` argument that its signature requires, so the tag's calibration script could not have run as is; the paper's runs used a slightly different state of the code (unknown). The mass-balance function itself is the one ported. PyGEM ≥ 1.0 (PyGEM-Community, MIT) is a rewrite on OGGM flowlines and was not used.
- **Agreement.** Qualitative: the S-shaped response of B to T_bias (Fig. 2), the non-identifiability (pp. 179, 182) as a one-dimensional active subspace, and the signs of the posterior correlations (p. 182; of the two reported sizes, only k_p–f_snow is similar). The paper's equifinal settings are glacier-specific and do not carry over.

2. Lapse rates: the paper's come from ERA-Interim pressure levels (p. 176); a constant −6.5 K km⁻¹ is used.
3. The exact study period and its indexing (t1, t2) of the paper's calibration: taken as the 18 calendar years 2000–2017. The tag's `pygem_input.py` sets custom dates (June–May), whose role in the paper's runs is unknown.
4. The exact code state of the paper's runs (see "Code version").
5. Regional priors (supplement Figs. S1–S2) were not used; the box is this data set's choice (table above). The size of the eigenvalue gap and the box-normalized weights depend on it (λ₁/λ₂ from 25.8 to 115.2 over the three boxes of the notebook); the direction per physical unit does not, to a few degrees. Eq. 18 as printed makes σ_reg a variance (or is a misprint for β = μ_reg/σ_reg²).
6. The ranges of d_prec and T_snow in study m5 are this data set's choice; the paper fixes both.
