# Energy

Batteries, photovoltaics, wind, nuclear, power generation, power grids and building energy use.

Each data set below has a short summary of the science: what the model computes, what varies, and what the active subspace shows. The numbers come from the data set's notebook, which has the details and the comparison with the paper. The [top-level README](../README.md#data-sets) has the paper links and the kind of each data set (stored data or Python model).

### [BISON_Fuel_Performance_Surrogates](BISON_Fuel_Performance_Surrogates/)

Radaideh and Kozlowski ran the BISON fuel-performance code on Case 1 of the OECD/NEA pellet-cladding mechanical interaction (PCMI) benchmark, a beginning-of-life power ramp of a 10-pellet PWR rod, with 13 fabrication, power and coolant parameters drawn from independent normal laws. The notebook estimates the active subspaces of four outputs from the 400 stored runs with a global quadratic fit. The fission gas produced, the maximum fuel centreline temperature and the radial cladding displacement are one-dimensional to within the noise of the fit (λ₁/λ₂ = 2919.7, 1210.1 and 1873.4; a quadratic in one active variable has leave-one-out R² 0.9994, 0.9973 and 0.9978); the centreline temperature is driven by the linear heat rate, the fuel porosity and the cladding surface temperature, and the fission gas is exactly proportional to pellet height times linear heat rate. The maximum fuel surface temperature needs a second direction, the pellet height (λ₁/λ₂ = 19.0, λ₂/λ₃ = 6.84; leave-one-out R² 0.9401 for a quadratic in two active variables). The pellet height acts on both temperatures through a non-smooth term, a raised window of pellet heights, that no quadratic captures: a fit linear in the other 12 inputs plus a piecewise-linear function of the pellet height raises the leave-one-out R² of the surface temperature from 0.9181 to 0.9839.

### [European_LV_Feeder_Probabilistic_Power_Flow](European_LV_Feeder_Probabilistic_Power_Flow/)

Ni, Nguyen and Cobben propagate the uncertain demands of the 55 single-phase loads of the IEEE European LV Test Feeder through an unbalanced three-phase power flow (OpenDSS) at the on-peak minute 09:26, for three outputs: the phase-A voltage at bus 899, the current in line 78 and the feeder loss. The notebook rebuilds the paper's base scenario (55 Gaussian load powers) and its 110-input scenario (with uniform power factors), with central-difference gradients through OpenDSS at 1000 Monte Carlo samples per case. All three outputs have a one-dimensional active subspace. In the base scenario λ₁/λ₂ is 2.2×10⁶ for the voltage, 8.5×10⁶ for the current and 4.0×10³ for the loss, but these very large ratios reflect a response that is nearly linear in the load powers: w₁ is the least-squares slope direction (|cos| = 1.0000000 for the voltage). The 110-input scenario, with λ₁/λ₂ of 549 to 2531 and visible curvature in the power factors, is the more informative case; there a function of the one active variable has leave-one-out R² of at least 0.998.

### [HTGR_Microreactor_Control_Drums](HTGR_Microreactor_Control_Drums/)

Price, Radaideh and Kochunas ran 751 Serpent Monte Carlo calculations of the 22 MWt HOLOS-Quad gas-cooled microreactor with its eight control drums turned to random angles, and recorded k_eff and the four quadrant powers. Because the drum angles are periodic, the notebook works in the drum insertion variables uᵢ = cos θᵢ, in which a global quadratic fit gives one dominant direction for k_eff (λ₁/λ₂ = 18.8), weighting all eight drums nearly equally with the four side drums 19 % heavier. The quadrant-1 power fraction also has one dominant direction (λ₁/λ₂ = 35.7), which sets the quadrant's own drums against the opposite quadrant's, and the other three quadrants repeat this pattern after the permutation the core symmetry predicts (|w·w′| ≥ 0.9993). Quadratics in one active variable reach leave-one-out R² = 0.949 (k_eff) and 0.951 (P₁). In the raw angles the same runs show no active subspace (all eight k_eff eigenvalues within a factor 1.56), and a linear fit explains nothing (R² = 0.01, as in the paper).

### [Lillgrund_FLORIS_Wake_Model](Lillgrund_FLORIS_Wake_Model/)

van Beek, Viré and Andersen calibrate the six wake parameters of the FLORIS Gauss-Curl Hybrid model (near-wake length α, β; wake expansion k_a, k_b; natural deflection a_d, b_d) against SCADA data from the 48-turbine Lillgrund offshore wind farm, and find with Sobol' indices that k_a, b_d and α dominate. The notebook runs the same FLORIS 2.0.1 model, ported to numpy, on a public Lillgrund layout with a generic turbine curve, and computes the active subspace of the total farm power at 8 m/s with wind from 180° (turbines in lines 4.8 D apart along the wind), from 500 uniform samples of the six parameters with finite-difference gradients. The farm power has a three-dimensional active subspace (an eigenvalue ratio of 156 after the third eigenvalue): a combination led by α, one led by k_a, and b_d; a cubic in the three active variables has leave-one-out R² = 0.975. Inactive are a_d, a contrast of k_a and k_b that leaves the wake expansion rate nearly unchanged, and a contrast of α and β that leaves the near-wake length nearly unchanged. Measured by first-order Sobol' indices, the model reproduces the paper's trends with turbine spacing for k_a (rising) and α (falling); the main difference from the paper is a much smaller index for b_d.

### [Lithium_Ion_Battery](Lithium_Ion_Battery/)

Hadigol, Maute and Doostan simulated a LiC₆/LiCoO₂ cell with Newman's porous-electrode model, with 19 uncertain inputs (porosities, Bruggeman coefficients, lengths, diffusion coefficients, conductivities, reaction rates and particle sizes of the anode, separator and cathode, and the Li⁺ transference number), in 3600 runs at each of three discharge rates, 0.25C, 1C and 4C. Constantine and Doostan study the capacity as a function of voltage and the voltage as a function of time; with no gradients, the notebook finds one direction at every voltage and time from a global linear fit and checks it with the eigenvalues of a global quadratic. At all eighteen points checked, the first eigenvalue of the quadratic model exceeds the second by 0.74 to 3.53 orders of magnitude, and the straight-line R² of the summary plots is between 0.840 and 1.000, the weakest case being the capacity at 1C. Porosities, lengths and Bruggeman coefficients are the most important inputs at 0.25C and 1C, and for the capacity at 4C; for the 4C voltage the separator porosity leads early in the discharge and the salt diffusion coefficient near its end (component −0.59). The notebook follows how the weights change with voltage and time and measures their bootstrap standard errors (at most 0.0099).

### [Lithium_Ion_DFN_Drive_Cycle](Lithium_Ion_DFN_Drive_Cycle/)

The Doyle–Fuller–Newman (DFN) model of a lithium-ion cell (PyBaMM, Marquis et al. parameter set) is run under a scaled US06 drive cycle at 650 Monte Carlo samples of the 24 uncertain parameters of Zonta et al., recording the terminal voltage every second for 600 s. Active subspaces of the time-averaged voltage and the end-of-cycle voltage are estimated from the values with a global quadratic fit, cross-checked with global linear and local linear estimates. The time-averaged voltage has one leading direction, made up mostly of the positive electrode's electronic conductivity and solid diffusivity, with λ₁/λ₂ ≈ 5 and a second gap of similar size; a one-variable surrogate predicts it with R² ≈ 0.7 in leave-one-out with the subspace held fixed, and 0.66 in 10-fold cross-validation that re-estimates the subspace in each fold. The end-of-cycle voltage needs two active variables (leave-one-out R² 0.51 with one, 0.69 with two). This disagrees with the paper's Sobol' analysis, which finds the conductivity and diffusivity negligible and ranks the positive electrode's thickness, porosity, particle radius and capacity first; the disagreement is unresolved.

### [Lithium_Ion_Impedance_Calibration](Lithium_Ion_Impedance_Calibration/)

Hallemans et al. identify the parameters of a lithium-ion cell from impedance data with the single particle model with electrolyte (SPMe), with double-layer capacitance, written in grouped parameters. The notebook computes the paper's least-squares impedance misfit Φ (nine states of charge, 60 frequencies from 200 µHz to 1 kHz) against noise-free synthetic spectra at the paper's true parameters, at 1000 Monte Carlo samples of the 18 grouped parameters, with exact adjoint gradients. The output log₁₀ Φ has a one-dimensional active subspace (λ₁/λ₂ = 23.6, bootstrap 16.9–32.4), made of the series resistance and the two charge-transfer time scales, which carry 90 % of trace(C); a cubic in that one variable predicts log₁₀ Φ with leave-one-out R² = 0.91. At the true parameters the leading eigenvector of the misfit's Gauss–Newton matrix is 5.9° from w₁, and the weakest directions of both lie in the span of the electrolyte-diffusion groups, which the paper found hard to identify, and t⁺, which the paper estimated well. Both the local (Cramér–Rao) and the global rankings follow the errors of the paper's best fit, the local one more closely (Spearman 0.88 against 0.59).

### [Offshore_Wind_Turbine_Fatigue](Offshore_Wind_Turbine_Fatigue/)

NREL's wind turbine simulator FAST computed the fatigue loads of a floating offshore wind turbine for 50,000 Monte Carlo samples of five wind and wave conditions: hub-height wind speed, significant wave height, wave direction, peak spectral wave period and wind direction with respect to the platform. The seven outputs are damage equivalent loads (DEL) at the blade root, the tower base and the three mooring-line anchors; the data have no gradients, so the notebook estimates them by local linear fits at 2,000 points. The two blade-root moments are nearly functions of the wind speed alone (w₁ is the wind-speed axis, λ₁/λ₂ = 212.5 and 117.0), and so, to a lesser degree, is the tower-base fore-aft moment (λ₁/λ₂ = 11.6, with the wave direction second). The three anchor tensions have a two-dimensional subspace, the plane of the platform direction and the wind speed (λ₂/λ₃ = 14.8, 20.4 and 20.2), and only the tower-base side-to-side moment has an oblique direction, mixing wind speed and wave direction, but its first two directions are not well separated (λ₁/λ₂ = 1.3). Quintic polynomials in the active variables reach leave-one-out R² from 0.9833 (blade-root in-plane moment) down to 0.7757 (anchor 1 tension). The active-subspace analysis is not published; the source paper uses the data to compare grid-based and Monte Carlo estimates of long-term fatigue loads.

### [PyPSA_Eur_Investment_Ranges](PyPSA_Eur_Investment_Ranges/)

Neumann and Brown solved the PyPSA-Eur capacity-expansion linear program of a fully renewable European electricity system for 500 Halton samples of five capital-cost multipliers (onshore wind, offshore wind, solar, hydrogen storage, battery storage) with a 37-node model, and for 15 of them with a 128-node model. The total annual system cost of the 500 least-cost runs has a one-dimensional active subspace: local linear gradients give λ₁/λ₂ = 26.32 (95 % bootstrap 21.97–30.55), with weights w₁ = (0.841, 0.174, 0.181, 0.436, 0.199) led by onshore wind and hydrogen storage. A quadratic in the active variable predicts the cost with leave-one-out R² = 0.9665 (0.9868 with a second variable). Four independent routes agree on w₁ to |wᵀw₁| ≥ 0.9968: a global quadratic, gradients from the envelope theorem of the linear program, the gradients of the authors' published polynomial-chaos surrogates, and a linear fit to the 15 high-fidelity runs. The squared weights reproduce the paper's first-order Sobol' indices of system cost to within 0.03.

### [Residential_Building_Stock_Energy_Use](Residential_Building_Stock_Energy_Use/)

Ali et al. simulated the annual energy use of about one million Irish dwellings with jEPlus and EnergyPlus; the notebook takes the 49,298 runs of semi-detached houses under historical weather without renewables, in which the energy use intensity (EUI) depends on 15 numeric inputs, each taking between 4 and 34 discrete levels. Gradients estimated from linear fits to the 60 nearest runs give λ₁/λ₂ = 39.65 and one active variable led by the HVAC efficiency (−0.807) and the hot-water use (+0.422), on which a cubic has leave-one-out R² 0.8286. The eigenvalues after the first are dominated by the variance of those local fits, but a weaker smooth structure (the directions w₃ and w₄) raises the R² to 0.8658, close to the 0.8664 of a quadratic in all 15 inputs. The stored runs have no EUI of 650 kWh/(m² yr) or more; a version of the data rebuilt without that cutoff turns the leading direction only slightly (|cos| 0.9934 for the linear fit) but has a leading eigenvalue 4.57 times larger. The simulated space heating is not monotone in the wall and roof U-values, which behave like labels of construction templates rather than physical conductances.

### [Single_Diode_Solar_Cell](Single_Diode_Solar_Cell/)

The single-diode model describes the current-voltage relationship of a photovoltaic cell; its output here is the maximum power P_max, a function of five inputs: the short-circuit current, the diode reverse saturation current (log-uniform), the ideality factor, and the series and shunt resistances. Constantine, Zaharatos and Campanelli analyze 10,000 runs of Campanelli's MATLAB code, with finite-difference gradients. P_max has a one-dimensional active subspace (λ₁/λ₂ = 19.4, λ₂/λ₃ = 8.0), weighted on the short-circuit current, the saturation current and the ideality factor, while the two resistances have components close to 0 over their ranges. A cubic in the one active variable has leave-one-out R² = 0.981. Sobol' indices also rank the same three inputs as important, but they reduce the dimension from 5 to 3, while the active subspace reduces it to 1.
