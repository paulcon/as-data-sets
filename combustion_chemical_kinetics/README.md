# Combustion and chemical kinetics

Combustion, flames, thermoacoustics and chemical reaction mechanisms.

Each data set below has a short summary of the science: what the model computes, what varies, and what the active subspace shows. The numbers come from the data set's notebook, which has the details and the comparison with the paper. The [top-level README](../README.md#data-sets) has the paper links and the kind of each data set (stored data or Python model).

### [Ammonia_Syngas_Optimized_Kinetics](Ammonia_Syngas_Optimized_Kinetics/)

Li, Zou and Yao optimized the 123 Arrhenius parameters of 41 influential reactions in a 37-species ammonia/syngas mechanism. Here the 41 rate constants vary uniformly over temperature-dependent uncertainty regions of the paper's shape and width, and Cantera computes the ignition delay at three conditions: a dilute shock-tube mixture at 1.4 atm and 1850 K (A), stoichiometric NH₃/air at 20 atm and 1400 K (B), and a rapid-compression-machine mixture at 40 bar and 1100 K (C), with finite-difference gradients at 100 Monte Carlo samples. At every condition one direction dominates the 123-dimensional input space (λ₁/λ₂ = 32.0, 8.5 and 13.7), and a quadratic in that single active variable predicts log₁₀ τ with leave-one-out R² 0.9448, 0.9138 and 0.9278. The three conditions are controlled by different chemistry: at 1850 K the direction is led by NH₃ + H = NH₂ + H₂, and at the lower temperatures the weight spreads over the NH₂ + NO, NH₂ + NO₂ and H₂NO + O₂ reactions.

### [Annular_Combustor_Thermoacoustics](Annular_Combustor_Thermoacoustics/)

Bauerheim et al. model the first azimuthal thermoacoustic mode of an annular combustion chamber with 19 burners fed from a common plenum; the complex eigenfrequency solves a nonlinear dispersion relation, and the output is the larger growth rate of the mode, with the risk factor the probability that it is positive. The gain and time delay of each burner's flame are uncertain, 38 inputs in all, in a weakly and a strongly coupled operating point, and a numpy implementation of the dispersion relation gives exact gradients at 1000 samples per case. The clearest gap is after the third eigenvalue (λ₃/λ₄ = 13.23 in the weak case), and five (weak) or six (strong) eigenvalues exceed 1 % of the largest, close to the paper's five. The first eigenvector weights the 19 time delays equally in the weak case, and gains and delays both in the strong case; a change of variables to the spatial Fourier coefficients F̂(k) of the burner terms Fᵢ = nᵢ exp(jω⁰τᵢ) reduces the leading directions to F̂(0) and F̂(±2), as the theory predicts. A quadratic in three active variables has leave-one-out R² 0.966 (weak) and 0.931 (strong), and the notebook uses the surrogates to estimate the risk factor (15.33 % for the weak case from the model's Monte Carlo runs, against the paper's 14.77 %).

### [H2_Flame_Kinetics_Bayesian_Calibration](H2_Flame_Kinetics_Bayesian_Calibration/)

Bell et al. calibrate 31 pre-exponential factors and third-body efficiencies of the HO₂ and H₂O₂ reactions of a hydrogen mechanism against 91 flame-speed, flow-reactor and ignition experiments. Here 500 Monte Carlo draws from the paper's broad truncated-Gaussian prior are run through Cantera for one ignition delay and two laminar flame speeds (H₂/air at φ = 1 and 1 atm, and at φ = 0.4 and 20 atm), with finite-difference gradients. The ignition delay (λ₁/λ₂ = 18.1, leave-one-out R² 0.9182 for a quadratic in y₁) and the lean 20 atm flame speed (λ₁/λ₂ = 6.5, R² 0.8788) each have one dominant direction, led by the low-pressure rate of H + O₂ (+M) ⇌ HO₂ (+M). The stoichiometric 1 atm flame speed has a two-dimensional active subspace (λ₂/λ₃ = 9.6, bootstrap interval [8.6, 11.1]): that rate, and the branching HO₂ + H → 2 OH against HO₂ + H → H₂ + O₂; a quadratic in the two active variables raises leave-one-out R² from 0.6972 to 0.9266. Its first eigenvalue is set by a handful of samples with the low-pressure rate near zero.

### [H2_NH3_Combustor_CFD_NOx](H2_NH3_Combustor_CFD_NOx/)

Piscopo et al. ran 175 2-D axisymmetric RANS simulations (Ansys Fluent, Partially Stirred Reactor (PaSR) turbulence–chemistry model, Shrestha NH₃/H₂ mechanism) of a staged rich–lean ammonia–hydrogen combustor over three operating conditions: the rich-stage equivalence ratio φ₁, the H₂ fraction of the fuel x_H₂ and the pressure p₀. The outputs are the outlet NOₓ (ppmvd at 16 % O₂) and the outlet temperature, and with no gradients the subspaces come from a global quadratic fit over the 173 distinct cases. NOₓ has a dominant direction that is almost −φ₁ (λ₁/λ₂ = 30.4) and a second, mostly pressure, direction (λ₂/λ₃ = 8.1), a plane that the bootstrap fixes only loosely. The best polynomial in these two active variables has leave-one-out R² 0.905, against 0.726 for one variable and 0.928 for all three inputs. The outlet temperature, which the energy balance was meant to hold at 1200 K, varies from 1194.8 to 1224.3 K along one direction, mainly x_H₂ (λ₁/λ₂ = 80.6, one-variable leave-one-out R² 0.959).

### [H2_O2_Ignition_Delay_Kinetics](H2_O2_Ignition_Delay_Kinetics/)

Vohra et al. compute active subspaces of the ignition delay of a fuel-rich H₂/O₂ mixture (φ₀ = 2, 1 atm, 900 K) with respect to the uncertain rate parameters of a 19-reaction mechanism: 19 log pre-exponential factors, and 36 inputs when activation energies and the initial state are added. The notebook rebuilds the study in Cantera with the same reactions and ranges and estimates the subspaces from 1000 Monte Carlo samples with finite-difference gradients in each case. The ranking of inputs matches the paper's: A₉, A₁, A₁₅ and A₁₇ for 19 inputs, and A₉, T₀, A₁, A₁₅, A₁₇ and E_a,15 for 36. But λ₁/λ₂ is only 6.7 and 9.6, with a second, sharper gap after λ₂ (λ₂/λ₃ = 56 and 107), and the second direction, led by the H₂O₂ reactions, describes the slow branch of ignition. The ignition delay switches between a slow branch (tens to hundreds of ms) and a fast one (about 0.1–1 ms) as k₁ overtakes k₉, which a one-dimensional quadratic captures only to a leave-one-out R² of 0.90–0.93; a cubic in two active variables reaches 0.9766 for 19 inputs.

### [NH3_H2_Kinetics_Uncertainty](NH3_H2_Kinetics_Uncertainty/)

Huang et al. quantify and reduce the uncertainty of a new NH₃/H₂ combustion mechanism (42 species, 346 reactions) by sampling the pre-exponential factors of 52 highly sensitive reactions within their initial uncertainty bounds. The notebook reruns the mechanism in Cantera at one shock-tube ignition condition (10 atm, 1587.98 K) and one laminar flame (50 % NH₃ + 50 % H₂ with air at φ = 1 and 1 atm), with gradients with respect to the 52 log₁₀ factors, on Latin hypercubes of 1000 (ignition delay τ) and 200 (flame speed S_u) samples. The flame speed has a clear one-dimensional active subspace led by H + O₂ ⇌ O + OH (λ₁/λ₂ = 10.22; a quadratic in y₁ has leave-one-out R² 0.9332). log₁₀ τ has one well-determined direction (λ₁/λ₂ = 6.14), but it leaves about a quarter of the variance (leave-one-out R² 0.7376) spread over many NHₓ/NO reactions.

### [Shared_Subspace_H2_Ignition_Flame](Shared_Subspace_H2_Ignition_Flame/)

Ji et al. combine the first eigenvectors of several combustion outputs into one shared direction that reproduces each output's distribution. The notebook rebuilds their hydrogen case in Cantera with Konnov's 33-reaction mechanism and its uncertainty factors, and computes 16 ignition delays and 5 flame speeds with gradients at 350 samples of the Gaussian log rate constants. Every output has a one-dimensional active subspace (λ₁/λ₂ between 51 and 1124), and a quadratic in y₁ has leave-one-out R² of at least 0.9712 and above 0.99 for 19 of the 21 outputs. The leading reactions change with the regime: chain branching by H + O₂ = OH + O leads most ignition delays, the H₂O₂ reactions lead beyond the second explosion limit, and H₂ + OH = H₂O + H leads the flame speeds; the first eigenvectors are far from parallel (cosines down to 0.095). One shared direction still approximates all 21 outputs, including a held-out condition, about as well as each output's own eigenvector does.
