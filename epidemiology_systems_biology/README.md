# Epidemiology and systems biology

Disease transmission and in-host or cellular dynamical systems.

Each data set below has a short summary of the science: what the model computes, what varies, and what the active subspace shows. The numbers come from the data set's notebook, which has the details and the comparison with the paper. The [top-level README](../README.md#data-sets) has the paper links and the kind of each data set (stored data or Python model).

### [CityCOVID_Chicago_Calibration](CityCOVID_Chicago_Calibration/)

CityCOVID is an agent-based model of COVID-19 in Chicago with 2.7 million agents. Robertson et al. ran it at 700 parameter sets of four calibrated inputs, each with 50 random seeds, and the data are the seed-averaged daily hospital census and cumulative deaths from 2020-03-02 to 2020-06-04. The notebook studies the cumulative deaths on 2020-06-04; there are no gradients, so the subspace comes from a global quadratic fit. It is one-dimensional (λ₁/λ₂ = 43.94; a quadratic in the one active variable has leave-one-out R² 0.9858) and dominated by the exposure rate θ₁ (weight 0.834) against the stay-at-home probability θ₃ (−0.441). The seeding-time input θ₂ acts as a step function, and on the 200 parameter sets inside the paper's prior range it drops out of the direction (weight −0.006).

### [COVID19_Exit_Strategies_virsim](COVID19_Exit_Strategies_virsim/)

virsim is a stochastic individual-based SEIR model of one million people, which Gugole et al. use to compare four COVID-19 exit strategies: Flattening the Curve, Contact Tracing, Intermittent Lockdown and Phased Opening, each with 6 to 8 uncertain inputs. The outputs are two intensive-care (IC) quantities: the peak of the 30-day moving average of IC patients per million, and the summed excess of daily IC patients over a capacity of 109 beds per million. Each strategy was run on a 512-point Sobol' design, with seed replicates to measure the noise, and the subspaces come from a global quadratic fit, without gradients. Every strategy has one dominant direction (λ₁/λ₂ from 10.4 to 16.0), led by its main policy inputs (uptake, intervention effect, tracing rate) and, except for Contact Tracing, by R₀. By one rule for all strategies, Flattening the Curve and Intermittent Lockdown have a two-dimensional and Contact Tracing and Phased Opening a three-dimensional active subspace, Contact Tracing's spanned mostly by its three tracing inputs. Cubic fits on these subspaces have leave-one-out R² from 0.903 to 0.973, limited only slightly by the seed noise, which is 0.2 to 3.9 % of the output variance.

### [COVID19_SEIR_Epidemic_Peak](COVID19_SEIR_Epidemic_Peak/)

Jensen, Engsig-Karup and Knudsen model the early COVID-19 epidemic in Denmark with a SEIR model and propagate the uncertainty in three inputs, the basic reproduction number and the incubation and infectious periods, to the time and the size of the epidemic peak. The notebook reimplements the model with exact forward-sensitivity gradients, agrees with the paper's means, standard deviations and Sobol indices, and estimates the subspaces in log coordinates from 1000 samples. Both outputs have one-dimensional active subspaces (λ₁/λ₂ = 1179 for the peak time and 1065 for the peak size), and a quadratic in the one active variable has leave-one-out R² above 0.999 for both. Because of a time-scaling symmetry, the peak size is exactly a function of two variables. The squared weights of the first eigenvector agree with the paper's 1000-run Sobol indices within 0.0013.

### [CovidSim_Cumulative_Deaths](CovidSim_Cumulative_Deaths/)

CovidSim is Imperial College's individual-based COVID-19 simulator. Edeling ran it at 3000 Monte Carlo samples of 51 uniformly distributed inputs (intervention, disease, place and spatial-kernel parameters), and the output is the cumulative death count after 801 simulated days. There are no gradients, so the subspace comes from a global quadratic fit, checked against linear and local-linear estimators. One direction, dominated by the delay from the end of the latent period to symptom onset, has λ₁/λ₂ = 15.69 and gives a linear fit with leave-one-out R² 0.897. A second direction, mostly the proportion of symptomatic cases and the social-distancing spatial contact rate, raises the held-out R² of a polynomial ridge fit from 0.903 to 0.953, which matches the paper's choice of a two-dimensional deep active subspace.

### [Ebola_SEIR](Ebola_SEIR/)

Diaz et al. model the spread of Ebola in Western Africa with a modified SEIR model that adds hospitalized patients and the infectious, improperly buried dead. The output is the basic reproduction number R₀, a closed-form function of eight parameters, varied uniformly over ranges for Liberia and for Sierra Leone; the matrix of averaged gradient products is computed with a tensor-product Gauss–Legendre rule of 8⁸ points. The notebook uses two active variables, as the paper does (λ₁/λ₂ = 3.9 and λ₂/λ₃ = 10.5 for Liberia; 7.1 and 7.1 for Sierra Leone), and a quadratic in them has leave-one-out R² 0.9380 for Liberia and 0.9328 for Sierra Leone, against 0.7765 and 0.8036 with one. The hospitalization rate ψ is by far the most important parameter in both countries, and more so in Sierra Leone (activity score 0.689 against 0.522), while β₂, ρ₁ and ω have little influence. This suggests that additional resources in either country should go towards increasing the hospitalization rate.

### [EMT_Switch_ODE](EMT_Switch_ODE/)

Gasior compares global sensitivity methods on a two-equation model of E-cadherin and Slug in the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), with 11 rate and half-saturation constants varied by ±10 % and ±25 %. The notebook reimplements the model with exact implicit-function gradients for one treatment group, two cells with TGF-β on, where every sampled draw is monostable, and studies E-cadherin and Slug at t = 10,000 min from 2000 draws per box. On the ±10 % box each output has a one-dimensional active subspace (λ₁/λ₂ = 227 and 174.5), and a polynomial in the active variable has leave-one-out R² 0.99753 and 0.99417. E-cadherin falls with the half-maximal contact constant IC_C and the E-cadherin degradation rate β₁ and rises with the membrane-translocation rate k₀, the three inputs of the paper's subset for this group; Slug rises with Slug production α₂ and with IC_C and falls with Slug degradation β₂. The normalized activity scores agree with the Sobol' total indices within 0.008, and the leading directions hardly turn on the ±25 % box.

### [HIV_Long_Term_Dynamics](HIV_Long_Term_Dynamics/)

Loudon and Pankavich's model of HIV has seven coupled ODEs for the CD4⁺ T cells, actively and latently infected T cells, macrophages, infected macrophages, cytotoxic lymphocytes and virions, and captures all three stages of the infection: acute, chronic and AIDS. Its 27 parameters vary by 2.5 % about their nominal values, and the output is the T-cell count at 19 times up to 3400 days, with finite-difference gradients at 1000 samples. The T-cell count has a one-dimensional active subspace at every time (λ₁/λ₂ = 3954 at 24 days and 874 at 3400 days, smallest 30 at 1600 days), although at 1600–1800 days the eigenvalues single out a second direction. The important parameters change with time: K₁ matters in the acute and chronic stages, and K₄ in the chronic and AIDS stages, where K₄, δ₄ and δ₇ carry the largest weights. Seven parameters have |w₁| ≤ 0.0013 at all 19 times and could be fixed at their nominal values for the whole course of the disease.

### [HIV_Three_Stage_Reservoir](HIV_Three_Stage_Reservoir/)

Clarke and Pankavich study a five-equation within-host model of HIV in which infected macrophages act as a long-lived reservoir, so that one set of constant parameters produces the acute stage, the chronic stage and the transition to AIDS. The notebook varies its 15 parameters over the paper's ±10 % box and computes exact forward-sensitivity gradients at 1000 samples of three outputs: the T-cell count at 5 and 10 years and the time to AIDS. Each has a dominant one-dimensional active subspace (λ₁/λ₂ = 103, 43.5 and 127.7), and the first eigenvectors match the paper's published weights with cosines 0.9990, 0.9996 and 0.9921. The leading parameters are δ_V, p_T (virus production by infected T cells), δ_T* and k_T, joined at 10 years and for the time to AIDS by the macrophage proliferation rate ρ_M. A cubic in the one active variable has leave-one-out R² 0.9898, 0.9594 and 0.9929; the 10-year count, near zero for most samples, also has a well-separated second direction.

### [Malaria_Bed_Net_Reproduction_Number](Malaria_Bed_Net_Reproduction_Number/)

Bala and Gimba build an eight-compartment human–mosquito model of malaria with drug-sensitive and drug-resistant strains, insecticide-treated bed nets (ITNs) and treatment, and derive its effective reproduction number R_eff in closed form. The notebook codes R_eff with its exact gradient in the 20 parameters of the paper's partial rank correlation coefficient (PRCC) figure, and reproduces the paper's five leading PRCC parameters in the paper's order. On 1000 Latin hypercube samples the active subspace is one-dimensional (λ₁/λ₂ = 10.6), led by ITN efficacy, human recruitment and the minimum biting rate. The direction is stable, but the ratio rests on few samples: the 10 with the largest gradients hold 47 % of the trace, and without them λ₁/λ₂ = 16.1. Because R_eff is heavy-tailed, a one-variable surrogate reaches a leave-one-out R² of only about 0.82.

### [MAPK_Cascade_Bistable_Oscillatory](MAPK_Cascade_Bistable_Oscillatory/)

The MAPK (RAF/MEK/ERK) cascade is modeled by three ODEs for the phosphorylated RAF, MEK and ERK concentrations, with negative and positive feedback from ERK; Linden, Kramer and Rangamani rank its seven free rate parameters with Sobol' indices in a bistable and an oscillatory regime. The notebook computes forward-sensitivity gradients on 512-point Sobol' designs of the paper's uniform ranges. For the bistable ERK state x₃ at 30 min, a few samples with a deactivation rate near its lower bound 0 dominate: the 5 largest of 512 gradients carry 90 % of the trace, and λ₁/λ₂ falls from 10.37 to 1.59 without them, so on these ranges x₃ has no robust low-dimensional active subspace. The limit-cycle amplitude, on the 171 oscillating samples, has λ₁/λ₂ = 5.7, led by k₂ (activity score 0.51), as in the paper. When each parameter is instead drawn log-uniformly from U/100 to the paper's upper bound U, x₃ has a one-dimensional active subspace that weighs the activation rates k₁, k₃, k₅, α against the deactivation rates k₂, k₄, k₆ (λ₁/λ₂ ≈ 12; a cubic surrogate has leave-one-out R² 0.87).

### [Minnesota_COVID19_Deaths](Minnesota_COVID19_Deaths/)

Wycoff, Binois and Wild test a method for learning active subspaces sequentially on the Minnesota COVID-19 model, for which they report a three-dimensional active subspace from a Gaussian process fitted to 2,000 random runs. The notebook ran the same R model at 100 Monte Carlo samples of its eight inputs, with forward-difference gradients (900 runs), and estimates the active subspace of cumulative deaths directly from the gradients. The eigenvalues drop by a factor of 352 after the third (λ₁/λ₂ = 9.04, λ₂/λ₃ = 3.34). The three active directions lie almost entirely in the span of the proportion of asymptomatic infections and the hospitalization and home-death probabilities of the oldest groups. The three-dimensional subspace is within a distance of 0.0152 of the one in the article's own reference matrix, and so confirms its choice of three dimensions.

### [STAT5_Dimerization_Signaling](STAT5_Dimerization_Signaling/)

The model of Boehm et al., one of the benchmark ODE models of intracellular signaling collected by Hass et al., describes how the hormone Epo drives the phosphorylation, dimerization and nuclear shuttling of the transcription factors STAT5A and STAT5B. The notebook samples its 6 kinetic rate constants uniformly in log₁₀ within one decade of the published best fit and computes exact gradients, by forward sensitivity equations, of the time-averaged relative phosphorylation of STAT5A at 1000 samples. The output has a one-dimensional active subspace (λ₁/λ₂ = 13.5) that combines heterodimer import, Epo degradation and phosphorylation, with a sigmoidal sufficient summary plot; a second active variable raises the leave-one-out R² of a quintic only from 0.9697 to 0.9812. Two of the six rate constants have no visible effect in this box. Over time the direction changes: early on phosphorylation alone controls the output, and from 20 min on heterodimer import leads.

### [Zika_SEIR_SEI_Brazil](Zika_SEIR_SEI_Brazil/)

Dantas, Tosin and Cunha Jr calibrate an eight-equation SEIR–SEI model of humans and *Aedes* mosquitoes to the weekly probable cases of the 2016 Zika outbreak in Brazil, and name an active-subspace study of the model as future work. The notebook does that study: it varies six transmission and progression rates and four initial conditions uniformly over the paper's calibration bounds and computes exact forward-sensitivity gradients at 1000 samples of two outputs, the cumulative number of cases at the end of 2016 and the least-squares misfit to the weekly data. The logarithm of each has a two-dimensional active subspace (λ₁/λ₂ = 7.25 and λ₂/λ₃ = 5.90 for the cases; 7.87 and 8.48 for the misfit), on which a cubic has leave-one-out R² 0.972 and 0.955. The first direction, led by the human recovery rate, the mosquito death rate and the two transmission rates, is close to the gradient of the basic reproduction number (|cos| = 0.938). The second is mostly the initial proportion of infected mosquitoes: in this box, the mosquitoes infected at the start of 2016 drive the size of the outbreak, not the initial human cases.
