## 1. Summary
SU2 flow and (continuous) adjoint study of the Lockheed N+2 supersonic passenger jet (the boom mesh with active engines), from Lukaczyk's dissertation (adviser Alonso; Constantine was on the reading committee).

The data set is confirmed as the dissertation's. With inputs scaled to [−1, 1] and each output min–max scaled to [−1, 1], its gradient eigenvalues reproduce fig. 4.14 (p. 108; §10). The output ranges and active-variable ranges match fig. 4.15 (p. 109). The data are rich (310 runs × 105 inputs × 3 QoIs with gradients), and they are the only 3-D supersonic and boom case in the collection. Recommend **include in main**, gated on the author confirming the release (Q1).

Notebook problems:
- It says 50 inputs; the data and code have 105. 50 is the main-wing count in fig. 2.20 (likely origin of the error).
- It cites the wrong paper (the 2014 ONERA-M6 AIAA paper).
- The adjoint gradients are visibly inaccurate for lift. Lift is exactly linear (full OLS R² = 1.000), yet the gradient-based w₁ is about 25° from the OLS direction.
  - The dissertation gives the mechanism but does not quantify it for N+2. On NACA0012, the SU2 continuous-adjoint gradients carry a bias of about 10 % for lift and 2 % for drag (Table 3.4, p. 72), "because SU2 surface based adjoint approach relies on a central differencing scheme with artificial dissipation constants that were calibrated for the drag gradient" (§3.3.2.1, p. 73).
  - The dissertation's GPR noise model exists to handle exactly this (abstract; conclusions p. 165).

## 4. Code review
- Cell 4: the slices `data[:,:105]`, `105:108`, `108:213`, `213:318`, `318:` assume the index column `Run` was dropped by `from_csv`. With `np.loadtxt` everything shifts by one (§5).
- Cell 6: `xl = -0.3*np.ones((1,m))` uses 2-D bounds. v1 takes `(m,)` arrays.
- Cell 10: `ss.compute(df=G)` passes the raw gradients while X is normalized. The gradients are in physical units: scaled by 0.3 (inputs) and by 2/range(f) (outputs), they reproduce dissertation fig. 4.14 (§10). So the legacy eigenvalues lack the 0.3² factor and are not comparable with fig. 4.14. **W is unaffected** because all ranges are equal. In v1, pass physical G with the physical space and v1 applies the 0.3.
- Cell 12: `eigenvectors(ss.eigenvecs[:,:3])` has 105 components and no labels. Use index ticks every 10.
- Cell 14: `partition(2)` is hard-coded for every QoI, yet the eigenvalues favor n = 1 for lift and EA. Pick n per QoI and show the 1-D and 2-D plots.
- `out_index = 2` selects a single QoI. Loop over all three instead, since execution is cheap (4 s total in the prototype).
- `nboot=200` is unseeded. Pass `rng=`.
- No hard-coded paths.
