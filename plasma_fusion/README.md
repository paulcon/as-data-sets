# Plasma and fusion

Plasma physics and fusion energy models.

Each data set below has a short summary of the science: what the model computes, what varies, and what the active subspace shows. The numbers come from the data set's notebook, which has the details and the comparison with the paper. The [top-level README](../README.md#data-sets) has the paper links and the kind of each data set (stored data or Python model).

### [EU_DEMO_PROCESS_Major_Radius](EU_DEMO_PROCESS_Major_Radius/)

The fusion systems code PROCESS sizes a tokamak power plant by solving one constrained optimisation: here, the smallest plasma major radius R₀ that still delivers 400 MW of net electric power with a 2-hour burn. The paper's input deck is not published, so the data are 64 optimisations of a related model, PROCESS v3.4.2 on its public large-tokamak example edited to the paper's six uncertain inputs: H-factor, divertor limit, core radius, tungsten fraction, elongation and helium fraction. From the 62 converged runs and a global quadratic fit (no gradients), R₀ has a one-dimensional active subspace: the first eigenvalue holds 96.5 % of the trace and the rest lie at the fit's noise floor. The direction, dominated by the H-factor and the elongation, is stable under leave-one-out and other estimators, but the size of λ₁/λ₂ is not determined by these runs. A quadratic in the one active variable predicts R₀ with leave-one-out R² 0.973.

### [Hall_Thruster_Multi_Component](Hall_Thruster_Multi_Component/)

Marks et al. couple a cathode-coupling model, the 1-D fluid discharge code HallThruster.jl and a semi-empirical plume model of a Hall thruster, and compute Sobol' indices over the prior of 18 model parameters. Here the coupled model of the SPT-100 was run 440 times on a Latin hypercube over that prior at one of the paper's operating conditions (300 V, 12.3 µTorr-Xe), and active subspaces of the corrected thrust T_c and the discharge current I_D come from a global quadratic fit to the 420 runs whose discharge does not oscillate. T_c depends mainly on one direction, dominated by the plume's zero-pressure divergence angle c₃ (λ₁/λ₂ ≈ 18; a cubic in the active variable has leave-one-out R² 0.921). I_D has a two-dimensional subspace of the anomalous-transport scale α_anom and barrier depth β_anom (λ₂/λ₃ ≈ 10; leave-one-out R² 0.959 with two variables, 0.844 with one). The eigenvalue ratios still rise with the number of runs, so they are lower estimates; the dimensions and directions are settled, and the leading inputs rank as in the paper's Sobol' indices.

### [JAG_ICF_Implosion_Scalars](JAG_ICF_Implosion_Scalars/)

JAG is a semi-analytic inertial confinement fusion (ICF) implosion simulator of Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, and Anirudh et al. released 10,000 of its runs to train neural-network surrogates. The runs have five inputs and fifteen diagnostic scalars, none named and none with units (inputs x₁, …, x₅, outputs s₀₀, …, s₁₄). Without gradients, local linear fits on all 10,000 rows find one dominant direction for the first output s₀₀: the gap λ₁/λ₂ depends on the number of neighbours per fit (9.19 with 8, 24.41 with 40) and is 18.1 from the gradients of a global degree-6 polynomial, while the direction barely moves, and a one-variable polynomial on it has leave-one-out R² 0.9758. In all fifteen scalars the first direction lies in the plane of x₂ and x₃. Several outputs also depend on x₅: for s₀₃, s₀₇ and s₀₉ a polynomial in (x₂, x₃) alone has leave-one-out R² of only 0.8658–0.9269, and adding x₅ raises it to 0.9874–0.9974.

### [MHD_Generator](MHD_Generator/)

Glaws, Constantine, Shadid and Wildey connect active subspaces with dimensional analysis on two magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) models with the same five log-inputs (viscosity, density, applied pressure gradient, resistivity and applied magnetic field) and two outputs, the average flow velocity u_avg and the induced magnetic field B_ind. The Hartmann problem, laminar flow between parallel plates in a perpendicular magnetic field, has closed-form solutions and exact gradients: both outputs have a two-dimensional subspace that describes them exactly (λ₁/λ₂ = 29.8 for u_avg and 13.6 for B_ind, the later eigenvalues zero to rounding), because each depends on only two dimensionless groups. The idealized 3-D MHD generator duct was computed with Sandia's Drekar code, with adjoint gradients, at 483 Monte Carlo samples. There u_avg is one-dimensional (λ₁/λ₂ = 31.2; one active variable leaves out 3.85 % of the eigenvalue sum), led by viscosity and pressure gradient as in the Hartmann problem. B_ind needs two (one leaves out 12.97 %, two 2.63 %), and dimensional analysis bounds the dimension of both by four.

### [Vlasov_Poisson_Instability_Growth_Rate](Vlasov_Poisson_Instability_Growth_Rate/)

Terrab and Pankavich measure how the growth rate γ of electrostatic instabilities in a collisionless plasma, from the dispersion relation of the linearized 1-D Vlasov–Poisson system, depends on the parameters of the equilibrium. The notebook reimplements the dispersion relation in Python with a certified search for the most unstable root and exact implicit-function gradients. In the global study of a Bi-Maxwellian equilibrium (six inputs, 1000 samples) the eigenvalues fall off without a large gap: the first holds η₁ = 74.6 % of the sum and the first two η₂ = 92.8 % (paper: 75.09 % and 93.58 %), w₁ weights the beam weight β and the wavenumber k most, and the two-dimensional subspace is 3.3° from the paper's. The Two-Stream, Double Beam and Bump-on-Tail studies vary the inputs by a percentage around a baseline (512 samples each); one active variable is enough up to 10–20 %, depending on the study, and two are needed from 15–25 %. The paper's Bump-on-Tail numbers are reproduced only with a different baseline and, from 10 % on, with the stable samples (γ ≤ 0) left out.
